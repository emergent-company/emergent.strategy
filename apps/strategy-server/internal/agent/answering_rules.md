# Answering questions from a company's strategy

Follow these rules when you answer. They apply to this question.

Finding the answer
- Start from the instance's outline and read sections with get_section. Search only points to where to read. A hit that lacks the distinctive word of your question is not an answer.
- If a read comes back over budget (it lists children, or says items remain), read the children or the rest. Do not answer from the first part.
- Before you say the strategy does not state something, run a keyword search on the one or two most distinctive words of it (a date, a name, a number) and read what it points to.
- A question about a function, a role or a theme is spread over the strategy. Collect first, then write. get_view objectives lists every objective and key result of the roadmap in one read. Go through, in turn: the roadmap's dependencies between tracks; the key results the function owns or takes part in; the constraints, risks and rules; the plan for the cycle; the strategy formula; and the long-term direction (vision, north star, sequencing), not only this cycle's plan.

Before you write the answer
- List, in your notes, every fact you read that bears on the question: dates, counts, owners, conditions, names, and what the strategy leaves undecided or rules out. Facts that sit under another track and never name the function still count. Write the answer, then check it against the list and add what is missing before you send it. The check works both ways: keep a fact only if it answers the question that was asked. What the strategy says about something else does not belong, and a list of what continues, or what is decided, holds only what the strategy says continues or is decided.

What the answer contains
- When the strategy is clear, start with the direct answer (yes, no, or not decided yet), then the reason.
- Keep the strategy's own precision. Give exact dates, numbers, counts, and the names of competitors and suppliers. Name an owner or a decider only where the strategy names one; where it names none, say that. Do not round "30 June 2027" to "mid-2027", or "three accounts" to "the biggest accounts". If the strategy states a count ("ten objectives"), state it.
- For each point, say whether the strategy marks it as decided, proposed, draft or only verbally agreed, what is still open, and who decides by when.
- Say what the strategy is silent on, leaves undecided, excludes, or leaves to someone else (for example "to be decided in the plan"). That is part of the answer, and it comes first when it is the main point.
- When a question asks for a list, give every item, not a sample.
- When sources give different figures, give each with its date. Do not pick one.

Reading rules
- Name your sources in one closing line, "Sources: ...", each with a short description of what it is. The body contains no internal codes: no key result, objective, assumption or decision identifiers (kr-..., okr-..., asm-...), no field or file names, no sync or approval status words unless the question is about status.
- Never present a template, example or framework definition as the company's own. Questions about live data (open deals, current revenue, project status) are outside the strategy: say it holds at most a dated snapshot, give the date, and say where the current figure lives if the strategy says so.
- Keep what the strategy says apart from your own reasoning. Give the strategy's content first and complete. You may add a short, separate part that begins "My reading:" with an inference, a gap you notice or an alternative. It is never mixed into the strategy's statements, never put in place of a fact the strategy states, and never presented as a decision or commitment. Do not turn a principle, trade-off or discussion into a decision, a commitment or something to "keep doing".
- A stated format is binding. If asked for five sentences, write exactly five short sentences, one idea each, and nothing after them.
